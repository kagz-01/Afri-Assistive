# Investor note

A briefing you can say out loud. It is not a term sheet. Numbers are the ones in the pilot unless marked as coming from the venture papers.

## The line

AfriAssistive keeps an assistive device in use. We do not count it as success when it leaves the workshop.

## The problem

Programmes report devices issued. A chair that is the wrong size, missing a bearing, or given away still sits in that number. The person is back to being carried, or out of school, or out of work.

In this pilot a community wheelchair costs KES 22,000. The listed SHA tariff is KES 15,000, and only if that budget is released. The KES 7,000 gap is real. Putting it on a claim would be false. A paediatric brace has no listed tariff, so the full KES 7,500 is a grant or a household payment.

The venture papers cite an unmet need on the order of 550,000 devices. This pilot has not measured that. A single county cohort is enough to test the question funders should ask: at 12 months, how many devices are still in use?

## What exists today

A working pilot, in English and Kiswahili, with synthetic Kenyan households.

- A fit lock. Six checks, or the device does not leave.
- A parts twin. The repair stays open while the failed spare is at zero.
- A tariff-gap ledger. The claim pack is blocked when the fit is open, the device is not handed over, or no tariff exists.
- Follow-up at 2 weeks, 6 months, and 12 months. Answers are still using, broken, or given away. Broken opens a repair.
- A QR passport with no name on the public face.
- Seven roles on four jobs: household, referrer, clinic, workshop, repair hub, county, and ops. The referrer cannot sign. The hub cannot release a new device. The county total hides a count small enough to name a household, and the partner’s device list has no owner name.
- A sample care-plan receipt. Paybill `880155`. No money moves.

You can click through all of that in one sitting. Reset the pilot records first.

## What does not exist yet

Say this before they ask.

- No contracted facility, no SHA submission, no live M-Pesa, no WhatsApp, no USSD short code.
- No staff logins and no shared database. The role switcher is a demo.
- A second phone cannot scan the QR and see the record. The registry is local until a server exists.
- No ODPC registration. Do not type a real patient into this build.
- The wheelchair’s standard is named as ISO 7176. That mark is not in hand.

## Who pays, for what

| Payer | Pays for | Does not pay for |
|---|---|---|
| Public insurer, once a facility is contracted | The listed tariff, where one exists | The gap, and devices with no tariff |
| County, donor, or disability fund | The gap, follow-up, spares, loaners | A software story with no outcome |
| Household | A care plan for service. Sample prices are KES 300, 500, and 800 a month | Assumed to cover a KES 22,000 chair |
| Rehab department | One register for the day’s work | A second spreadsheet |

Worked example, not a forecast. One hundred community chairs leave **KES 700,000** the listed tariff does not cover, before clinicians, visits, loaners, and spares. The listed price in the pilot is KES 26,000 against a cost of KES 22,000. That KES 4,000 is not spendable margin while the gap is unfunded.

The recurring line is the care plan. It becomes revenue only when a real paybill collects it.

## The round we should be having

Not a software multiple. A first site.

1. Register the company. Register with the ODPC. Write the data-protection assessment before any real name.
2. One rehab department or county hospital, in writing. Twelve months. The result is still-in-use, time to repair, and the gap in shillings.
3. If we manufacture the chair, walk the standards path before the partner accepts one.
4. Fund the cohort’s gap, the follow-up, and a small spare float. A smaller cohort than one hundred is a better first contract.
5. Only then: a paybill, a shared record, staff accounts, and a payer conversation with SHA.

Talk to the county health team and the rehab department first. An “AI platform” room is the wrong room. The product does not make a clinical decision.

## Five minutes in the room

Reset the pilot. The role name is in the top bar. Then, in this order:

1. **Partner / payer.** County board. Still in use, overdue checks, the unfunded gap, open spare orders. Point at the gap. Say it is not a claim. The device list shows serial and county, not the owner’s name.
2. **Workshop.** A spare at zero. Show that the repair cannot close. Show a job that cannot be released until QA is complete. Switch to **Repair hub** only if someone asks: that radio has no release button.
3. **Clinician.** Clinic day. A device that cannot be handed over. Tick the six checks only if you want them to see the lock open. Otherwise leave it locked. The community referrer does not have this screen.
4. **Passport.** The QR, the serial, and the sentence that the name is not on the page.
5. **Claims.** A pack that stays blocked. A clinician or a partner can open it. The referrer cannot.

Stop there. Do not open the paybill and call it revenue.

## Sentences not to say

- “SHA reimburses the chair.”
- “Households pay on M-Pesa today.”
- “We already integrate with WhatsApp and USSD.”
- “Scan this code on your phone.”
- “We have users.” These are sample records.
- “AI decides who receives a device.”

## What is hard to copy

Not the screens. The rule that the record and the money agree: no handover without a fit, no closed repair without the part, no claim for a gap the tariff does not pay, and no success number other than the device still being used. A catalogue or a WhatsApp group can look busy and still hide abandonment.

## Ask, when you are ready to name one

Price it from the first site, not from a valuation story.

- Legal and data-protection setup, before a single real record.
- One partner’s clinician time for 12 months.
- The unfunded gap on a small cohort, plus a spare-and-loaner float.
- The standards path if the chair is ours to make.

Bring this pilot. Bring the gap in shillings. Bring the 12-month question. Do not bring a promise the screens already refuse to make.
