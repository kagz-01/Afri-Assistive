# AfriAssistive — innovation, importance, and commercial viability

Reference note for the founder. Written 22 October 2026 against the working pilot, not against a live clinic.

This is not a legal opinion, not audited accounts, and not a claim that SHA, Safaricom, or a county has contracted the service. Figures below are the ones the pilot already uses. Where a number comes only from the venture papers, it is marked as such.

## What the system is

AfriAssistive is the record of an assistive device from referral to the question that matters a year later: is the person still using it?

The pilot follows the service chain used in the product: referral, assessment, prescription, funding, preparation, fitting, training, and follow-up. Each step can stop the next one. A device is not handed over on an unsigned fit. A repair is not closed without the spare that failed. A claim is not written as if SHA will pay a gap it does not list.

The public face of a device is a QR passport. It carries the model, serial, status, and warranty. It does not carry the owner’s name, location, or health information.

## Why it matters

Most assistive-technology programmes are counted when the device leaves the store. That number does not say whether the chair is still in use, stuck for a bearing, or given away. Abandonment is the failure this system is built to see.

Three facts make that failure expensive in Kenya, using the pilot’s own prices:

| Device | Cost to make or buy | Listed SHA tariff in the pilot | Unfunded gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Community wheelchair | KES 22,000 | KES 15,000 | KES 7,000 |
| Walking frame | KES 2,200 | KES 500 | KES 1,700 |
| Elbow crutches (pair) | KES 1,300 | KES 900 | KES 400 |
| Paediatric AFO | KES 7,500 | Not listed | KES 7,500 |
| ADL grip set | KES 300 | Not listed | KES 300 |

A listed tariff is not cash in hand. In this pilot it is only available if that SHA budget has actually been released. The gap is a grant, a household payment, or a device that should not be promised. Writing the gap into a claim would be false.

The venture papers cite an unmet need on the order of 550,000 devices. That figure is not re-measured here. Even a much smaller true number is enough to justify one county pilot, because the scarce thing is not another catalogue. It is a device that is fitted, repairable, and still in use at 12 months.

A person who abandons a chair is not a closed case. They are back to being carried, missing school, or missing work. The follow-up answers — still using, broken, given away — are the record a county or a funder can audit. A count of devices issued is not.

## What is innovative

These are product choices, not slogans. Each one is already in the pilot.

**The fit lock.** Six checks, all required: seat, footrest, brakes, pressure points, training in the person’s language, and a named caregiver or a note that the user is independent. Handover stays locked until they are ticked. That is when the 2-week, 6-month, and 12-month checks are written. There is no half delivery.

**The parts twin.** Every model names the spares that belong to it. A repair cannot be closed while the failed part is at zero. The workshop orders that part, receives it, and only then returns the device. A loaner can go out in the meantime, if one is on the shelf. This is how a flat tyre stops being a reason to abandon the chair.

**The tariff-gap ledger.** For each device the claim pack stores cost, listed tariff, and the gap. If there is no tariff, or the fit is unsigned, or the device has not been handed over, the pack is blocked. The county board shows the unfunded amount separately from anything that could be submitted. The innovation is the refusal, not the form.

**Follow-up as the outcome.** “Still using” is the success metric. “Broken” opens a repair. “Given away” is recorded as abandonment, not hidden inside “distributed.” Small counts on the county board are suppressed so a single household cannot be identified from a public total.

**The QR passport.** The token is minted when the device is released. The square is drawn in the browser from the page address only, in a few milliseconds, and nothing is uploaded to make it. A scan opens the public passport and can start a fault report. It is not an identity document.

**Four jobs, four boards.** The household sees their device, the next check, and any sample receipt. The clinic sees who is waiting, who cannot be handed a device, which care-plan prompt is unsent, and which check is overdue. The workshop sees dry spares, open repairs, and jobs that have not passed QA. The county sees still-in-use, the unfunded gap, and open spare orders. Nobody gets a generic dashboard with everyone else’s work on it.

**The fault line.** A person can report a fault from a simple phone-shaped screen or a desk shaped like a message thread. The pilot opens a ticket and can assign a loaner. It does not send a WhatsApp message or dial a USSD short code. Those channels are drawn so the flow can be tested. They are not live.

**Care-plan prompt and receipt.** The clinic sends a sample prompt. The household then sees a receipt number, paybill `880155`, the device serial as the account, and a 12-month schedule. The screen says it is not a live debit. No money moves.

**Language and access.** English and Kiswahili, a larger text size, and a higher-contrast mode are part of the product, not a later coat of paint. Training “in their language” is one of the six fit checks for the same reason.

## Commercial viability

The business is not “software seats.” It is a service that gets a device to a person and keeps it working, with software as the record. Four parties can pay, and they do not pay for the same thing.

| Who | What they would pay for | What this pilot already shows |
|---|---|---|
| SHA or another public payer | The listed tariff, only where a tariff exists and a facility is contracted | The claim pack, and the reasons it must stay blocked |
| A county, donor, or disability fund | The gap, the follow-up, the spares, and the loaners | The gap in shillings, and still-in-use versus given away |
| The household | A care plan for service, not the full price of the chair | Sample plans at KES 300, 500, and 800 a month |
| A clinic or rehab department | A way to run the day without a second register | The clinic board and the fit lock |

Unit economics for a first site, using the wheelchair as the worked example:

- One hundred community chairs leave KES 700,000 that the listed tariff does not cover (100 × KES 7,000), before clinician time, follow-up visits, loaners, and spares.
- The listed price in the pilot is KES 26,000 against a cost of KES 22,000. That KES 4,000 is not a margin you can take while the gap is unfunded. It disappears if the household cannot pay and no grant fills the KES 7,000.
- A brace with no listed tariff is entirely a grant or a household payment. Do not describe it as an SHA product.
- The monthly care plan is the recurring line. It only becomes revenue when a real paybill collects it. In the pilot it is a sample receipt.

What would make this commercially real, in order:

1. A registered company, then ODPC registration and a data-protection assessment, before any real name or phone number is typed in.
2. One clinical partner in writing, and a 12-month protocol. The result they agree to judge is still-in-use at 12 months, repair time, and the unfunded gap in shillings.
3. If the chair is manufactured, the standards path the model already names (ISO 7176, then the national mark). A partner should not accept a chair that has not passed it.
4. A first-site budget built from the gap, not from a software subscription. One hundred chairs is the illustration above. A smaller cohort is a better first contract.
5. Only after that: a Safaricom paybill, a shared record store, staff accounts, and a conversation with SHA as a payer. The current paybill, WhatsApp desk, USSD frame, and claim pack are demonstrations.

Who to talk to first: the county health team and a rehab department. A generic “AI platform” round is the wrong room. The product does not make a clinical decision. A clinician does.

What a serious funder can be shown today, on a reset pilot so the sample data is clean:

- The county board: still in use, overdue checks, and the unfunded gap.
- The fit lock: a handover that will not proceed.
- A repair that will not close while the spare is out.
- A claim pack that stays blocked when the tariff does not cover the cost.

What not to claim in a meeting:

- That paybill `880155` collects money.
- That a claim is submitted to SHA.
- That WhatsApp or USSD is connected.
- That a second phone can scan the QR and see the record. The registry is on the browser that created it until there is a shared server.
- That the role switcher is a staff login.
- That the unmet-need figure has been measured by this pilot.

## What this is worth protecting

The thing that is hard to copy is not the screens. It is the rule that the record and the money must agree: no handover without a fit, no closed repair without the part, no claim for a gap the tariff does not pay, and no success metric other than the device still being used. A catalogue, a WhatsApp group, or a spreadsheet of “devices issued” can look busy and still hide abandonment.

Keep the sample data synthetic until the registrations above exist. The importance of the system is the service. The commercial case is the gap plus the outcome. The innovation is that the software will not let you report one without the other.
